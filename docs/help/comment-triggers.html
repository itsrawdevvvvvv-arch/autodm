Keywords, matching, and what happens when nothing matches

5 min read · Updated 29 October 2026

Matching is deliberately simple and predictable: if a comment contains one of your keywords, the automation fires. Here's how to make that behave well.

How matching works

  • Matching is case-insensitive: Price and price are the same trigger.
  • Matching is substring-based: the keyword link also matches “link please” and “linkk”.
  • Each trigger can hold several keywords, and several triggers can watch the same post.
  • If two triggers match, the more specific one wins — the one with the longer matching keyword.

Handle the variations

Add the forms you actually see: price, pricing, cost, how much, kitna. Emoji-only comments are best handled with a catch-all rather than trying to enumerate them.

Use a catch-all carefully

A catch-all replies to every comment that didn't match anything else. It's useful on launch posts and dangerous everywhere else — nothing is more obviously automated than a generic reply to “love this”. If you enable one, keep the message short and human, and review the log weekly.

When nothing matches

The comment stays in your normal inbox, unanswered by the automation. That's intentional: guessing produces worse outcomes than silence. If the log shows the same unmatched comment repeatedly, that phrase belongs in your keyword list.

Public replies

Optionally, Viraloop can also post a short public reply under the comment (“sent to your DMs”). It's off by default because it changes the feel of your comment section — turn it on for launches, off for evergreen posts.
