The comment-to-DM playbook: writing a trigger that actually converts

14 October 2026 · 7 min read · Viraloop team

The mechanics of comment automation take ten minutes to set up. The copy takes longer, and it's the only part your audience ever sees. Here's how we'd approach it if we were starting from an empty account today.

1. Choose keywords people actually type

Watch your comments for a week before you build anything. You'll see the same five or six phrases over and over: link, price, how much, send it, info, and a pile of emojis. Those are your triggers — not the clever keyword you'd like people to use.

Two rules that save a lot of pain:

  • Add the misspellings. Matching is literal. If people type “prce” you either add it or you lose them.
  • One trigger, one destination. A keyword that sends two different links depending on the phase of the moon will confuse you more than your audience.

2. Write the DM like a person, not a receipt

The weakest DM in this category is a bare URL. It reads like an error message. The strongest is two sentences: what this is, and what to do next.

Here's the pricing sheet you asked for — three plans, no add-ons. If you want, reply “call” and I'll send a booking link.

Notice what that does. It delivers the thing, it sets expectations, and it offers a second, smaller action. That last part matters more than the first: people who won't buy today will happily take a next step that costs them nothing.

3. Give the DM somewhere to go

A conversation that ends with a link is a dead end. Decide what the next step is before you send anything: a booking page, a one-question reply, a saved post, a checkout. One step, not three.

4. Reply to the comment too, sometimes

A short public reply — “sent it to your DMs 🙌” — does two things. It tells everyone reading the comments that the account is alive and responsive, and it nudges the platform's own ranking signals. It's optional, but it's cheap.

5. Send exactly one follow-up

Twelve to twenty-four hours after the link goes out, if there's no reply, send one short nudge: “Did the link come through?” That's it. One. A second follow-up converts almost nobody and annoys everybody, and Instagram's own messaging rules give you limited room anyway.

What to measure

Ignore vanity numbers. Three things matter: how many comments matched a trigger, how many of those DMs were opened and replied to, and how many turned into the action you wanted. If replies are high but the final action is low, the problem is the destination. If replies are low, the problem is the DM copy. If matches are low, the problem is your keywords.

The one mistake to avoid: promising something in exchange for a follow. It's explicitly against Instagram's platform rules, it's the fastest way to get a tool — and an account — restricted, and it converts worse than a straight answer anyway.
