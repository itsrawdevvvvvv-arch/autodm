What Meta actually allows: a creator's guide to DM automation rules

12 September 2026 · 8 min read · Viraloop team

This is a summary written by a product team, not legal advice — always check Meta's current platform terms yourself. But if you're deciding what to build, these are the lines that matter most.

Rule one: only reply to people who spoke first

Automation is for answering, not for prospecting. Sending unsolicited DMs to people who never interacted with you is spam under Meta's rules and will get an account restricted quickly. Every legitimate tool in this category works the same way: a comment, a story reply or a direct message starts the thread.

Rule two: don't trade rewards for follows

Requiring someone to follow you before they can receive a link, a file or a discount is the pattern known as follow-gating. It's prohibited, it's the most common reason automation setups get shut down, and it's why we don't offer it — even though it's the single most requested feature we get.

What you can do instead: deliver the thing immediately, and ask for the follow as a friendly, unenforced suggestion in the same message.

Rule three: respect the messaging window

Instagram's messaging API is designed around a conversation that's already happening. Automated messages are expected to follow promptly from the user's last message. Anything that looks like a marketing blast to a quiet thread is what gets flagged — which is why we cap follow-ups at one by default.

Rule four: use the official API, not a browser trick

Tools that log in as you and click around the interface are violating the terms even when they work. They also break constantly, and when Instagram decides to enforce, the account that gets restricted is yours. Official API access means approved scopes and a connection you can revoke from settings.

What this means in practice

  • Automate the first reply, not the relationship.
  • Keep the copy honest and specific — no fake urgency.
  • Cap follow-ups, and stop them the moment someone replies.
  • Give people a way out: “reply stop” should actually stop things.
  • Log everything, so you can prove what was sent and why.

If a tool promises you the grey area, ask who carries the risk. The answer is always you: the platform restriction lands on your account, not on the vendor's.
